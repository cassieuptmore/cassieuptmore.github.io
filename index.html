The Build Playbook

How I scale event programs from zero.

The discipline that built the FarEye program from nothing — and the same discipline that lets a program clone to a new market in weeks instead of years. Four moves, in order.

01 — Audit & Architect

Where the program will earn its place.

Competitive landscape audit. Account targeting against ABM signals. Event scoring against pipeline goals. Most programs fail here because they pick events on instinct — I architect a portfolio that maps to revenue.

02 — Build the System

Not events. Infrastructure.

Vendor ecosystem stood up from scratch. KPI frameworks tied to pipeline, not vanity. Playbooks, run-of-shows, and SOPs documented the day they're written. By month six there's a system, not a series of one-offs.

03 — Run with Rigor

Production craft, on every show.

Real-time on-site leadership. Sales partnership embedded into every event. The 500 small decisions made right — because if the show falls apart, the system doesn't matter.

04 — Measure & Iterate

Programs that compound.

Pipeline attribution. ROI reporting that survives a CFO review. Post-event playbook revisions so the next program is sharper. The reason my FarEye playbook outlasted my tenure is this loop, not heroics.

The toolkit. I use modern tools — including AI — where they earn their place: competitive research, ABM personalization at scale, content multiplication, and post-event analysis. Tooling serves the playbook. Never the other way around.

Case 01 Pipeline Generation

Zero-to-One: $12M Influenced Pipeline in 24 Months

The Challenge

A Series C logistics SaaS company. No event program, no vendors, no playbook, no historical data. Sales asking events to deliver pipeline immediately. Marketing budget: $1M for the year.

The Approach

Built a tiered portfolio so the budget went to the highest-intent rooms first. Stood up the vendor ecosystem from zero. Integrated 6Sense intent signals with event registration data so every event tied to a target account list. Designed a custom GPT trained on our ICP, messaging pillars, and brand voice — the team used it for pre-event briefs, on-stand talking points, and follow-up. Built a post-event playbook and ran it after every program.

The Outcome

$12M influenced pipeline on a $1M budget in 24 months — 12× ROI. $4M sourced pipeline. 20+ programs/year running on a documented playbook a successor could run without me.

What it shows. I build event programs as revenue infrastructure, not a calendar.

Case 02 Portfolio Architecture

The Tier 1/2/3 Framework

The Challenge

Same SaaS company. With 60+ industry events on the calendar and a $1M budget, "go to all of them" wasn't an option. Sales wanted everything. Finance wanted nothing.

The Approach

Built a three-tier framework, ran every prospective event through it, and reviewed the portfolio quarterly with sales leadership. Tier 1 — Flagship Bets (4–6/yr, $80–150K each, 25+ meetings goal). Tier 2 — Targeted Plays (8–12/yr, $20–40K each, 10–15 meetings). Tier 3 — Field & Intimate (12+/yr, $5–15K each, deepen 10–20 named accounts). AI handled first-pass landscape audits and account-density scoring; I made the final call with sales VPs.

The Outcome

Cut the calendar from a proposed 40+ events to 22. Pipeline efficiency: +34% pipeline-per-dollar YoY. Quarterly portfolio reviews became standard — events were the only marketing channel sales asked for more of.

What it shows. Director-level event strategy isn't about doing more events. It's about making the portfolio defensible.

Case 03 Intent-Driven Field

Intent-Driven Field Marketing Engine

The Challenge

A global SaaS company's NA field team running monthly city dinners that filled seats but didn't move pipeline. Right format, wrong audience.

The Approach

Rebuilt the field engine around intent, not geography. Used 6Sense to surface in-market accounts city-by-city. Pre-event: a custom GPT trained on our ICP and messaging generated per-account executive briefs — title-level talking points, recent funding/M&A signals, conversation hooks. Field reps walked into every dinner with five minutes of relevant context per guest. Replaced "open invite" with intent-qualified guest lists. Built a 14-day AI-personalized, human-reviewed follow-up sequence mapped to the topics each guest engaged with.

The Outcome

3× pipeline per field event within two quarters. 47% of attended accounts advanced to next sales stage within 60 days (vs. 18% baseline). Sales reallocated budget to field events from other channels — first time in three years.

What it shows. Intent data + AI personalization + intimate format = the highest-ROI field play I run.

Case 04 Team & Global Ops

Building & Scaling a Global Event Team

The Challenge

Inheriting a one-person event function and being asked to scale it to NA + EMEA + APAC inside 18 months — without losing the quality bar that justified the investment.

The Approach

Org design first. Hired three full-time regional managers, each owning their portfolio end-to-end. On-demand contractor pools per region for production scale. Agency partners reserved for flagship moments. Unified playbook, regional adaptation. A master playbook (run-of-shows, vendor scorecards, KPI frameworks, brand standards) every region used. A custom GPT trained on our brand voice adapted copy to regional tone without losing the through-line. AI enablement from day one for every new hire.

The Outcome

Scaled to 40+ programs/year across 3 regions without proportional headcount growth. ~40 hrs/month saved on brand review alone. Promoted two of the three regional managers within 18 months.

What it shows. I don't just run programs. I build the team and the system that runs them when I'm not in the room.

Case 05 Audience-Mapped Flagship

The Audience-Mapped Flagship Journey

The Challenge

A global SaaS company's annual user conference — 4,500 attendees, three days. Last year's NPS was solid; pipeline impact was murky. CMO wanted one event to drive all GTM goals: pipeline, renewals, advocacy, analyst influence, partner pipeline.

The Approach

Rebuilt the conference as five parallel journeys sharing infrastructure, not one journey serving everyone badly. Mapped every registrant to one of six personas (Net-New Prospects, Active Opportunities, Customers, Champions, Partners, Analysts). Each persona got a designed journey — pre-event comms, on-site experience, follow-up sequence — owned by the right internal function (sales / CS / partnerships / comms / product marketing). A custom GPT auto-generated AE prep briefs for every active-opportunity attendee using CRM context + session signals.

The Outcome

$28M influenced pipeline in 90 days post-event (vs. $11M prior year, same content). NPS held at 72. 94% of attendees had a journey-defined "moment" they remembered (up from 41%). The journey map became the planning artifact for every subsequent flagship.

What it shows. I architect flagship events as multi-audience GTM machines. The customer journey isn't a brochure phrase — it's the operating model.
