The Thesis

Do more events — and do them well — by building the system, not by adding headcount

AI isn't a cost-cutter here — it's a revenue accelerator that gives a five-person team 10x–100x human leverage: the output of an organization many times its size. Doing more events and doing them well are not in tension. A system — not the headcount — is what produces quality at scale. Stack three layers of leverage and one person credibly runs a big-company program; what more budget and people buy later is not quality, it's parallelism and reach.

Operating Model

Two versions of the same machine — side by side

Start in Model A on day one to build the system and bank quick wins; graduate to Model B as pipeline proof unlocks budget. The system is identical — only the throughput changes.

"1 human, a pod of AI agents, 2 retainer vendors, the partner channel"

The human does only what a human must: strategy, relationships, on-site presence, exec dinners, judgment, saying no. Everything reproducible is delegated.

Sprint

PM agent CC'd on email + in meetings: tracks transcripts, updates the tracker, pings deadlines

What the solo model really delivers: a genuinely large, high-quality program — the flagship plus a full-calendar Tier 2 presence and a relentless field + dinner cadence (~25–30 Tier 3), all to a high standard, because the system (not headcount) guarantees the quality. The limits are not volume or quality — they're simultaneity (one person can't host three dinners in three cities the same night), deep geographic reach, single-point-of-failure risk, and the most bespoke white-glove production. Those four things — not "doing events well" — are the only things Model B's resources actually buy.

The AI Operating Model

How a small team produces big-company event & field assets

Not by chatting with a chatbot — by turning repeatable tasks into shared, governed workflows. Build the system once and it compounds.

The Marketing Brain

A versioned repository of prompts & "skills" encoding Prophix SOPs, brand, and frameworks. Anyone invokes an "executive-dinner" or "programmatic-SEO" skill and gets on-brand output in seconds. The difference between a clever individual and a scalable team.

Zero-to-One workflows

Automate the first 80%, humans finish the last 20%. From one event topic: auto-research guests & accounts, generate speaker summaries & titles, draft host talking points and the full invite + reminder sequence — in minutes.

Mass personalization

AI video (e.g. HeyGen) creates promos where a speaker thanks each registrant by name, at scale — an "unfair" level of high-touch engagement larger competitors can't match.

Agentic PM — "Sprint"

A bespoke project-manager agent CC'd on email and in meetings: tracks transcripts, updates the tracker, pings owners on deadlines. The program runs itself between standups.

Sales-enablement "brains"

An approved content library so sales generates bespoke, on-brand decks & outreach off every event in real time — closing the marketing-to-sales gap, no off-brand drift.

Content & SEO engines

Programmatic SEO spins up dozens of on-brand landing pages from real keyword data; a quarterly refresh keeps them ~3× more likely to be cited by AI search (Perplexity et al.).

AI as thought partner — CRIT

Not an oracle. The CRIT framework (Context, Role, Interview, Task) makes AI interview you one question at a time to surface gaps in the plan before a dollar is spent.

Built to last + the wizard ratio

Modular infrastructure swaps LLMs/agents as pricing shifts — no lock-in. And ~one technical builder per 10 marketers (a "vibe coder") turns ideas into working tools and custom event apps in hours.

Governance — a risk-level operating model. Categorize every AI task by brand risk. Low-risk, internal (monitor / summarize / report — the "Sprint" agent) runs autonomously. High-risk, customer-facing (personalized outreach, deal strategy, anything a prospect sees) always requires human-in-the-loop review — one obvious error there invalidates brand trust.

Feedback loops that keep it agile

Pre-event: target-list validation with Sales — kills wasted spend before it happens.
In-event: conversations + lead capture logged same day.
48-hour rule: every lead routed & followed up in two business days — non-negotiable SLA.
5-day retro: fast, blameless, written into the backlog.
Monthly pipeline review with Sales — what's converting, what's stuck.
Quarterly rebalance: double down on winners, kill underperformers, reallocate budget.

Sprint cycles — manufacture "noise in the market"

Run field marketing like product engineering. Replace the rigid annual plan with sprint cycles — 3-week and 2-month blocks — so the team is always shipping: a new field play, a local series, a personalized promo, a refreshed landing-page set. Constant shipping matches the speed of AI and creates a persistent "noise in the market" that makes a small team look larger and more innovative than rigid incumbents. Billion-dollar perception comes from visible, constant innovation — not one big annual bet.

Operating rhythm: Weekly standup + Kanban of every event by stage (the "Sprint" agent keeps it current) · sprint demos every 2–3 weeks · quarterly portfolio scoring & budget rebalance. Treat the event portfolio like a product backlog — each event is a scored bet; the calendar is living, not frozen in January.
