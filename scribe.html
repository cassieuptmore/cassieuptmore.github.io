Field Marketing Operating System

Get the room. Prove the pipeline.

A field marketing plan for Scribe Optimize — how to get CIOs and VPs of Finance into rooms they'd actually attend, and turn what happens after they leave into sourced enterprise pipeline.

Prepared by Cassie Uptmore for the Field Marketing Manager — Scribe Optimize role · A green-field program, built from scratch · 12-month horizon
The Thesis

The field motion should make the same promise the product does

Scribe Optimize sells enterprise leaders one thing: show me where AI will actually pay off — with data, not hype. The events function has to make that exact promise. Get the right buyers in a room, then prove sourced pipeline after they leave. Not booths. Not badge scans. Not attendance. Pipeline.

01

The room is earned, not bought

CIOs and VPs of Finance ignore generic invites and walk past booths. They show up for peers, a sharp point of view, and ROI they can take to a board. So the formats are intimate and high-signal by design.

02

The motion is half the event

The event is the easy part. The pipeline lives in the pre- and post-event motion: account targeting, exec-to-exec invites, multi-threading, and a follow-up cadence run shoulder-to-shoulder with BDRs and AEs.

03

The scoreboard is pipeline

Attribution is wired in from day one. Every format defends its cost-per-opportunity in the sales pipeline review — and anything that doesn't convert gets cut and reallocated. No vanity metrics.

The role, in one line: this program answers "how do we get those buyers in the room, and what happens after they leave?" — and holds itself accountable to sourced pipeline, not attendance. There's no playbook yet. Building it is the job.

The Sales Motion

The event is the easy part — the motion around it is where pipeline lives

Run shoulder-to-shoulder with BDRs and AEs. Every event has a job before the invite goes out and a job after the last handshake.

Before

  • Account targeting: BDRs + field pick named accounts by ICP × intent — not "anyone who'll register"
  • Invite strategy: exec-to-exec, personalized; the invite itself starts the multi-thread
  • Arm the rep: a pre-event brief on each attendee — their workflow pain, the Optimize angle, the ask

During

  • Every AE/BDR walks in with a per-attendee objective: a next meeting, a new thread, an exec sponsor
  • The content earns the room — a peer conversation and a board-ready POV, not a pitch
  • Capture signal in real time: who leaned in, what workflow pain surfaced

After

  • 24–48h follow-up, personalized to what was discussed — an Optimize workflow teardown, not a "thanks for coming"
  • Multi-threading playbook: BDR works the other stakeholders in each account
  • 30-day cadence, defined and logged; FMM gets the loop back on what advanced
Arming the rep is the difference. A great roundtable with a weak follow-up motion sources nothing. A good roundtable with a sharp pre/post motion compounds for a quarter. The playbook — invite templates, briefs, multi-thread maps, cadences — is a reusable asset, not a one-off.
Attribution

How we know events are working — and what changes when they're not

This is the part of the role that earns the seat in the pipeline review. Attribution is built with RevOps on day one, per-event and per-program.

What we measure

  • Sourced pipeline — net-new opps created from event accounts
  • Influenced pipeline — open opps touched by an event
  • Meetings → opps → closed-won — the full funnel from each event
  • Cost per qualified opportunity — trending down sprint over sprint
  • Velocity — event accounts vs. non-event accounts

What we don't

  • Attendance & registration counts
  • Badge scans and booth traffic
  • "Brand presence" as a success metric

These are inputs at most — never the scoreboard.

What changes when it's not working: every format has a kill criterion. If its cost-per-opp doesn't beat the program average over two cycles, it gets cut and the budget moves to what converts. That's a number I'll bring to sales leadership and defend — and revise out loud.

The First 90 Days

Building the playbook from scratch
