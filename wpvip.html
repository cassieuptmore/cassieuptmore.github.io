The Thesis

Experiential marketing is a revenue function — the events that earn their budget own pipeline end to end

WordPress VIP sells the enterprise content platform behind Meta, Salesforce, and Disney. The buyers are senior, skeptical, and hard to reach — so events aren't brand theater, they're a commercial instrument. Every event on the calendar should be designed backward from sourced pipeline, accelerated deals, and deeper customer relationships. Logistics are table stakes; the job is business outcomes, not activity for its own sake.

01

Designed backward from pipeline

Target account lists, ICP-fit invitees, BDR/Sales activation, and a follow-up motion are built before the SOW — not bolted on after. Every event has a number.

02

Operationally flawless

Agency, vendors, venue, AV, catering, swag, run-of-show and budget managed end to end, so problems get solved before an attendee ever sees them.

03

Measured in Salesforce

SAOs sourced, pipeline influenced, deals accelerated — tracked, reported, and fed back into the next event. The data decides what scales and what gets cut.

The operating idea: a small senior team plus AI can run a 30+ event global portfolio that punches far above its headcount — if the work is systematized, the pipeline motion is disciplined, and AI absorbs the production load so human judgment goes to strategy, relationships, and the room.

Every Event's Three Jobs

No event earns its budget on one metric

WordPress VIP's own brief says it plainly: events exist to generate pipeline, accelerate deals, and strengthen customer relationships. The system designs and scores every event against all three — plus thought leadership where it drives demand — so the portfolio always balances the number with what compounds.

Job 1

Generate pipeline

SAOs sourced and ICP-fit accounts engaged. The number the team is measured on.

Job 2

Accelerate deals

Open opportunities advanced — executive programs and customer moments that move late-stage deals, tracked in Salesforce.

Job 3

Strengthen relationships

Retention, expansion & advocacy with customers like the world's biggest brands — plus thought leadership that builds demand.

How the system uses this: every event is tagged with a primary and secondary job before it's greenlit, so the calendar stays balanced — enough sourced pipeline to hit the number, enough deal acceleration and relationship depth to compound. An event that can't articulate its job doesn't make the calendar.

The Pipeline Engine

Owning pipeline contribution end to end — not just logistics

This is the part of the role that earns the seat in the revenue conversation. Every event runs a disciplined pre / during / post motion, built with BDRs and Sales, and wired to Salesforce before the doors open.

Before

  • Define the target account list and opportunity goals with Sales
  • Prioritize ICP-fit attendees; intent-based outreach with BDRs
  • Salesforce campaign, lead capture & follow-up sequences set up before the event
  • Arm every rep with a per-account brief

During

  • Every rep walks in with a per-attendee objective
  • Capture intent signals and meetings in real time
  • On-site lead = run-of-show owner and problem-solver

After

  • Follow-up motion fires within 24–48h, by account
  • Report SAOs sourced, pipeline influenced, deals accelerated
  • Feed the data back to improve the next event's ROI
Answers the application question directly ("an event where you owned pipeline end-to-end, not just logistics"): pipeline ownership isn't a post-event report — it's an account list, a rep-activation plan, and a Salesforce-wired follow-up motion designed before the SOW is signed.
Run of Show

Operational excellence is the price of admission

Thirty-plus events a year, on time and on budget, means the operating system has to make complexity boring. Every event runs the same nine-stage lifecycle so nothing is invented twice and nothing is dropped.

End-to-end ownership

  • Agency relationship, timelines & production schedules
  • Vendor sourcing, venue, AV, catering, swag & shipping logistics
  • Contracts, invoices & expense tracking — on-budget, timely AP
  • Full scoping before SOW so staffing & logistics gaps are caught early

On-site leadership

  • Own the run-of-show; manage logistics in real time
  • Solve problems before they're visible to attendees
  • Single point of accountability, kickoff through post-event report
  • Surface risks early — with proposed solutions, not just problems
Answers the application question ("most operationally complex event you've managed"): complexity is handled by the system — a reusable lifecycle, vendor playbooks, and a PM cadence — so the senior hours go to the judgment calls, not the checklist.
The AI Operating Layer

Where AI adds leverage — and where human judgment stays

The role asks for a clear point of view on AI. Mine: point AI at the reproducible 80% — research, drafting, scoring, reporting — and keep humans on the 20% that's judgment, relationships, and the room. That's how a small senior team runs a big portfolio.

AI does the load

  • Account & attendee research; show-ROI scoring
  • First-draft invites, pages, briefs, recaps, follow-ups
  • Pulling Product Marketing narratives & customer insights into briefs
  • Budget tracking, dashboards, post-event reporting

Humans own the judgment

  • Which accounts, which formats, which rooms
  • The relationships and the on-site read
  • Final approval on anything customer- or brand-facing
  • Critically evaluating and refining every AI output (brief → critique → refine)
Answers the application question ("how are you using AI today"): not as an oracle, but as a briefed, supervised teammate — accelerating planning, research, and execution while a human makes the final call. The leverage is real (a small team produces like a much bigger one); the accountability stays human.
